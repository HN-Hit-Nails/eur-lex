4.1.      Elastomer coatings for automotive weatherstrip applications
(8)       The Transaction leads to one affected market for the supply of elastomer coatings
          for automotive weatherstrip applications (and for one plausible segment of that
          market). (5)
(9)       Automotive coatings are coating solutions that are applied to vehicle materials
          and components to provide functional surface performance and protection. Within
          automotive coatings, elastomer coatings for automotive weatherstrip applications
          (“elastomer coatings”) are specialty coatings applied as a thin layer onto
          automotive elastomer seals or plastic profiles (e.g. glass run channels, door
          profiles, trunk seals, etc.) to reduce noise, friction and vibrations, and to insulate
          the vehicle interior from humidity and dirt intrusion. These products are sold to
          original equipment suppliers (OES), who integrate them into the door, window
          and roof solutions they offer to original equipment manufacturers (OEMs).
(10)      Elastomer coatings comprise two main types of products: seal coatings and flock
          bonding solutions. Seal coatings are applied as an external coating layer on the
          elastomer substrate and provide a complete weatherstrip coating solution. In
          contrast, flock bonding involves applying short fibres onto a substrate that has
          been previously coated with an adhesive. The fibers determine the functionality
          of the flock bonding solution, while the adhesives enable the adhesion of the fibers
          to the substrate.
(11)      The Parties both manufacture and supply seal coatings, as well as flock bonding
          adhesives which, as explained in the previous paragraph, are just one component
          of the complete flock bonding solutions that compete with seal coatings.

4.1.1.2. The Notifying Party’s views
(14)    The Notifying Party submits that the relevant market comprises all elastomer
        coatings for automotive weatherstrip applications, including seal coatings and
        flock bonding solutions. Although these two product types are based on different
        technologies, the Notifying Party considers them to be largely substitutable since
        they serve the same core functions of reducing friction as well as noise and
        vibration, ensuring waterproof sealing, and improving the final appearance of the
        product. (9) At the vehicle design stage, OEM customers generally set
        performance criteria that elastomer coatings need to meet, rather than specifying
        a particular product type. In addition, all OES customers have the necessary
        production lines to use either technology when coating elastomer substrates. (10)
        The Notifying Party also argues that the substitutability between seal coatings and
        flock bonding solutions is high when considering their technical characteristics,
        prices, input materials, manufacturing process, and end-applications. (11)

4.1.1.3. The Commission’s assessment
(15)    The results of the Commission’s market investigation strongly suggest that
        substitution between seal coatings and flock bonding solutions is limited. From a
        supply-side perspective, while it is technically possible to switch production
        between the two types of products, many suppliers only produce one but not both,
        which contributes to different competitive dynamics. (12) From a demand-side
        perspective, the majority of customers regard seal coatings and flock bonding
        solutions as distinct product categories with different technical characteristics,
        manufacturing requirements and performance attributes. (13) In practice, the two
        technologies appear to be used mostly for different car parts, with seal coatings
        dominating static door and frame applications, and flock bonding being preferred
        for dynamic glass guidance applications. (14) Moreover, technical specifications
        from OEM customers often dictate the choice of technology. (15)

5.2.      Conglomerate links
(43)      In addition to elastomer coatings, both Parties supply a variety of complementary
          products used at different stages of the automotive production process. While the
          Parties’ combined market shares for these products do not result in horizontally
          affected markets, the Commission considered whether the Transaction could give
          rise to potential conglomerate effects due to the complementarity of these
          products.
(44)      Pursuant to the Non-Horizontal Merger Guidelines, (52) in most circumstances,
          conglomerate mergers do not lead to any competition problems. However,
          foreclosure effects may arise in conglomerate mergers when the combination of
          products in related markets gives the merging parties the ability and incentive to
          leverage a strong market position from one market to another closely related
          market by means of tying or bundling or other exclusionary practices.
(45)      In assessing the likelihood of a foreclosure scenario in conglomerate mergers, the
          Commission examines, first, whether the merged firm would have the ability to
          foreclose its rivals, second, whether it would have the economic incentive to do
          so and, third, whether a foreclosure strategy would have a significant detrimental
          effect on competition, thus causing harm to consumers. In practice, these factors
          are often examined together as they are closely intertwined. (53)
(46)      Stahl supplies both leather finishing coatings and performance coatings for non-
          leather flexible materials to OES who manufacture automotive interior parts.
          Henkel sells automotive adhesives that are also used in automotive interior parts.
          Stahl’s share of the EEA leather finishing coatings market is estimated at around
          [30-40]%, (54) while Henkel’s share of the automotive adhesives market is
          estimated at around [10-20]% for interior lamination adhesives and less than 5%
          for other adhesives used in automotive seats and interior trims. (55)

(49)    Based on the above elements, the Commission concludes that the merged entity
        will not have the ability to foreclose competitors. Given that the conditions for
        foreclosure are cumulative, there is no need for the Commission to assess whether
        the merged entity would also have the incentive to foreclose rivals, and whether
        such a strategy would have a significant detrimental effect to competition.
(50)    Therefore, the Commission concludes that the Transaction does not raise serious
        doubts as to its compatibility with the internal market or the EEA Agreement in
        terms of possible conglomerate effects.


6.      CONCLUSION
(51)    For the above reasons, the European Commission has decided not to oppose the
        notified operation and to declare it compatible with the internal market and with
        the EEA Agreement. This decision is adopted in application of Article 6(1)(b) of
        the Merger Regulation and Article 57 of the EEA Agreement.
